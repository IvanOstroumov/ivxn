Nvidia vs AMD gets argued like a permanent rivalry with a fixed winner. In practice, the better choice shifts by price tier and generation — brand loyalty is a weak signal.

## Compare within your actual budget, not across the whole lineup

<Callout>
"Which brand is better" is the wrong framing — check which specific card, at your actual target price, offers the best performance in reviews for that exact segment. The answer often differs at the budget, mid-range, and high-end tiers within the same generation.
</Callout>

## Factors beyond raw frame rate that genuinely matter

- **Ray tracing performance**, if you actually play games that use it meaningfully — Nvidia has historically had an edge here, though the gap varies by generation and specific title.
- **DLSS/FSR-style upscaling**, if the games you play support it well — these can meaningfully improve performance at a given visual quality, and the two ecosystems have different levels of game support and quality at different points in time.
- **VRAM amount for your resolution/use case** — a card with a strong price-to-performance ratio but insufficient VRAM for your target resolution and settings can bottleneck in specific titles, regardless of its raw compute performance.
- **Driver stability track record**, which varies by generation and shifts over time on both sides — worth checking recent (not years-old) driver reputation specifically.

## A reasonable comparison process

1. **Set your actual budget first**, not a brand preference.
2. **Check current-generation reviews specifically for that price tier** — not general brand reputation, not last generation's results.
3. **Weigh the features you'll actually use** (ray tracing, upscaling) rather than features that sound impressive but you won't use.
4. **Check current VRAM recommendations** for your target resolution and games.

## Comparison table (general tendencies — verify against current-generation reviews)

| Factor | Historical Nvidia tendency | Historical AMD tendency |
|---|---|---|
| Ray tracing performance | Often stronger | Improving, historically behind |
| Upscaling ecosystem maturity | DLSS, broad game support | FSR, growing support |
| Price-to-raw-performance at a given tier | Varies by generation | Often competitive or better at some tiers |
| VRAM at a given price point | Sometimes less generous | Sometimes more generous |

<Callout type="warning">
Every specific claim above shifts generation to generation — treat this as a framework for how to compare, not a current-generation verdict. Check reviews for the actual cards you're considering before buying.
</Callout>
