Not every recovery situation is the same, and treating them all the same way — always DIY, or always calling someone immediately — wastes either time or money in the cases where it's the wrong call.

## When DIY is genuinely reasonable

- **Simple accidental deletion**, noticed quickly, on a drive that's otherwise healthy and detected normally.
- **Data that isn't irreplaceable** — if the recovery attempt fails, the cost of that failure is low.
- **A formatted drive**, attempted with the drive not written to since (see our [formatted drive checklist](/blog/formatted-disk-first-steps)).
- **You're comfortable stopping** if the first attempt doesn't work, rather than escalating with increasingly aggressive tools on the same drive.

## When it's time to call a specialist instead

<Callout type="warning">
Clicking, grinding, or repetitive noises from a mechanical drive mean stop immediately — continuing to run the drive risks turning a professionally recoverable physical failure into a much harder one.
</Callout>

- **Physical symptoms** — noise, the drive not spinning up, not being detected by any computer at all.
- **A failed or partial RAID array** ([see our RAID failure guide](/blog/raid-array-failure-recovery)) — reconstruction mistakes here are hard to undo.
- **Legal, business, or evidentiary weight** — anything where a botched attempt has consequences beyond "the data is gone."
- **You've already tried DIY tools without success** — at that point, further attempts often have diminishing returns and rising risk, especially if any of them wrote to the affected drive.

## A simple decision framework

| Situation | Reasonable first move |
|---|---|
| Accidentally deleted a file, drive sounds/behaves normally | DIY recovery tool |
| Drive making noise or not detected at all | Stop — specialist, physical issue likely |
| Formatted drive, not written to since | DIY tool (PhotoRec-style file carving) |
| RAID array with 2+ failed drives | Specialist — reconstruction risk is high |
| Data has legal/business significance | Specialist, regardless of symptoms |
| Already tried 1-2 tools, no luck | Stop escalating — get a second opinion |

## The actual cost of getting this wrong

The real risk with DIY attempts isn't wasted time — it's that some recovery actions (writing recovered files back to the same drive, running an aggressive scan on a physically failing drive, attempting a RAID rebuild with guessed parameters) can make a situation that was genuinely recoverable into one that isn't. Recognizing which category you're in before you start is the highest-leverage decision in the whole process.
