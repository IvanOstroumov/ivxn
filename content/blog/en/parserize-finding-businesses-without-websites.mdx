Cold outreach usually starts from a vague pitch. Parserize started from the opposite idea: find a concrete, visible gap first, then the conversation writes itself.

## The core idea

Rather than pitching web development services broadly and hoping to find someone who needs them, Parserize identifies local businesses that have **no website at all, or one with clear, obvious problems** — outdated design, broken mobile experience, missing basic information. This turns a vague sales pitch into a specific, demonstrable observation: "I noticed your site doesn't work well on mobile" is a concrete conversation starter in a way "do you need a website?" never is.

## Why this approach actually works better than generic outreach

<Callout>
A cold pitch based on a specific, visible, checkable problem is fundamentally different from generic outreach — the prospect can immediately verify the claim themselves, which builds credibility before you've said anything else.
</Callout>

Generic "we build websites" outreach competes with every other generic pitch a business owner has already ignored. "Your site currently redirects to a broken page on mobile" is specific, verifiable, and hard to dismiss as spam.

## How the identification process works at a high level

1. **Query public business listing data** for a target category and area (restaurants, local services, whatever the relevant market is).
2. **Check for the presence and basic health of a website** — does one exist, does it load, does it render reasonably on mobile, does it have basic expected information.
3. **Flag the clear gaps** — no website, broken website, or missing fundamentals — as prioritized prospects, rather than treating every business as an equal-probability lead.

## Automating the checking, not the outreach

<Callout type="warning">
Automating the *identification* of a clear, checkable gap is a very different thing from automating unsolicited mass outreach — the latter has real legal and reputational risk (spam regulations vary by jurisdiction) that the former doesn't carry the same way.
</Callout>

Parserize's value is in surfacing genuinely good prospects efficiently — the actual outreach is still a real conversation, not a mass-blasted template.

## Where this generalizes beyond web development

The underlying pattern — find a specific, checkable, visible gap rather than pitching broadly — applies to other services too: a business with an outdated point-of-sale system, one with an obviously broken online booking flow, one missing basic accessibility features. The specific signal changes; the principle of leading with something concrete and verifiable doesn't.
