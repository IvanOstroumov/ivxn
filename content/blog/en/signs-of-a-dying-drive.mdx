Drives rarely fail with zero warning — the signs are just easy to miss or explain away. Here's what to actually watch for.

## HDD-specific warning signs

- **Clicking or grinding noises** — a mechanical hard drive making repetitive clicking sounds is one of the most reliable failure indicators there is. Stop using it and back up immediately if you hear this.
- **Increasing bad sectors** — files that suddenly won't open, or new corruption in files that were fine before.
- **Unusual slowdowns on file access** — the drive struggling to read specific areas, often just before those areas become unreadable entirely.

## SSD-specific warning signs

- **Sudden read-only mode** — some SSD controllers, when they detect they're near failure, switch the drive to read-only to prevent further data loss. If a drive that was previously writable suddenly can't be written to, back up what's readable immediately.
- **Increasing wear level in S.M.A.R.T. data** — SSDs have a finite number of write cycles per cell; a "media wearout indicator" approaching its limit is a genuine warning sign, not a false alarm.
- **Frequent, unexplained freezes or disconnects** — especially under write-heavy tasks.

## Shared warning signs (both HDD and SSD)

| Sign | What it usually means |
|---|---|
| Files becoming corrupted with no other explanation | Bad sectors or failing controller |
| Drive not recognized intermittently | Failing connection or controller |
| Noticeably slower boot/file access over weeks | Gradual degradation |
| S.M.A.R.T. warnings in disk utility tools | The drive's own diagnostics flagging a real issue |

## What to actually do when you notice a sign

1. **Back up immediately** — not "this weekend," now. A drive showing warning signs can fail completely with no further notice.
2. **Stop writing new data to it** if you're already suspicious — every write is now competing with limited remaining reliable life.
3. **Replace the drive**, don't just monitor it. A drive showing genuine S.M.A.R.T. warnings or physical symptoms has already told you its answer.
